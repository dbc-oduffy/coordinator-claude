**Triage lens — artifact-shape tradeoff.**

- _Artifact-shape tradeoff?_ (architectural direction, scope question, sequencing call, file-organization call, abstraction boundary)
  → Surface to PM with finding + reasoning. Wait for direction.
  Plan reviews skew heavily toward this row — most plan findings are about *what to build*, not *how it's coded*.
  - _(i) YAGNI / scope-trim argument from reviewer?_ → **Always escalation, never auto-trim.** Even framed as tradeoff-free, YAGNI is a product decision. Surface to PM. _See `coordinator/snippets/em-operating-doctrine.md` § How to Decide._
  - _(ii) Refactor-over-patch signal?_ → Refactor is the default when AI is the implementer. Surface to PM with refactor proposal. Under agent orchestration a critical rewrite is often an afternoon rather than a quarter, so *"too big"* means genuinely complex, not merely large. The cost asymmetry that makes patching look like the conservative choice does not hold here.
  - _(iii) Build-vs-defer call?_ → Always PM. Never EM-unilateral. _See `coordinator/snippets/em-operating-doctrine.md` § How to Decide._

**Triage lens — premise / hypothesis question (`--surface plan`).**

- _Premise / hypothesis question?_ (reviewer challenges the artifact's framing or motivating hypothesis, or claims a bug/gap exists where the artifact is correct)
  → Read the cited prior art (wiki, lessons, archived spec) at its source, not the reviewer's paraphrase. Confirm or revise premise either way.

**Triage lens — worker dispatch recommendations (`--surface plan`).**

- _Worker Dispatch Recommendations block present in reviewer output?_
  → On plan reviews: `doc-link-checker` (most relevant); `dep-cve-auditor` (if plan introduces a dependency). `test-evidence-parser` and `security-audit-worker` do NOT fire for plan reviews — they require runtime artifacts; if a reviewer names them on a plan, treat as miscalibration and surface to PM.

**Prior-Art Mutability and Reviewer Elevation**

**Prior-art mutability as first-class deliverable.** When the PM authorizes prior-art mutation as an explicit deliverable of the review — the plan is to *update* settled doctrine, not comply with it — the director-elevated reviewer (typically the Director of Engineering) MAY override the prior-art-checker sidecar's `update-plan` / `update-prior-art` recommendation with an explicit cross-reference to the PM authorization in their findings. Default mode is still "plan adapts to prior art"; prior-art mutation is opt-in and requires PM sign-off stated in the dispatch brief.

**Reviewer elevation must be stated verbatim in the dispatch brief.** Elevating a reviewer past their charter without verbatim brief language reverts at integrator-apply — the reviewer's prompt-defined charter is the default boundary. If the PM authorizes elevation, the EM must include the exact authorization phrase (e.g., "PM-authorized to override prior-art-checker on this run") in the dispatch brief to the reviewer; otherwise the integrator will treat the override as out-of-charter and escalate as ASK.

**Execution authorization gate.** When all reviewer integration is complete, surface to the PM and WAIT: _"Plan reviewed and integrated. Proceed to execute?"_ Review approval is not execution authorization — the EM does not begin dispatching on its own, and the write-bar is the PM's words, not their sentiment: the stamp op below fires ONLY when the PM's message NAMES execution ("execute", "proceed to execute", "run it", "go ahead and execute", or an unambiguous captain's-order "go" idiom — "make it so", "engage", "hit it", "let's fly"). Note "ship it" is deliberately NOT on this list — it is colloquially ambiguous (can mean merge/ship a diff, not execute a plan) and is judged by intent in context, the "ship it" that DOES fire a gate is the distinct review-WAIVER at reviewer selection ("ship it / skip review"). Plan-approval words alone ("looks good", "lgtm", "approved", "nice") authorize the PLAN, NOT its execution, and MUST NOT fire the stamp op.

**The deferral slate rides with this gate, argued not listed.** If the plan carries candidate scope cuts (rows `open` and carrying `case_against`, or already `backlogged`/`wont_do`), the same ask surfaces them alongside execution — per candidate cut, the case for, the case against, and the EM's recommendation with confidence. **Presenting the slate as an ID list ("ratify D1 to D3?") is the failure mode**: a ratify-prompt has no surface for the argument that changes the PM's mind. Above n=4 candidate cuts, the gate presents buckets and a spinoff proposal instead of an enumerated slate. The gate presents per-row arguments, never the derived bucket name: a grouping approval goes to the PM as its rows, never as the bucket name, because the bucket name is the EM's own derivation and approving it approves a summary nobody argued. Candidate-cut rows are authored `open` and flipped to `backlogged`/`wont_do` by `plan_tasks.mutate` only AFTER the PM rules here — the write-time guard already enforces that ordering; this gate is the ratification moment it is enforcing against.

**Precondition — land every plan-body edit the approval entails BEFORE invoking the op.** On a plan whose frontmatter carries a `grouping_approvals` key, that means the `do` / `defer` / `ruled_out` grouping block(s) the PM just approved: `status: approved`, the `approver`, `approved_at`, the PM's verbatim `pm_utterance`, and the membership `digest` for that grouping — plus the plan-header `Status:` line and any final integration note. **The `digest` is the one field in that block you never author from your own head.** The other four are the PM's words and the clock; the digest is a mechanical sha256 over the grouping's membership set, and a merely plausible value is worse than none — it binds the approval to a cut-set nobody approved, and the write-time guard rejects every later close against it. Take it from (POSIX-host form; PowerShell hosts: Shape W, `snippets/resolve-coordinator-bin.md`)
`& "$env:COORDINATOR_SETTINGS_HOME\bin\plan-tasks-grouping-digest.cmd" --plan <plan-path> --grouping do|defer|ruled_out --cut <id>:<disposition>[,<id>:<disposition>...]` — it prints the digest over the membership the cut-set produces, never from an estimate of what the recipe would say. Pass `--cut` for every row the PM's cut-set is closing (with its target disposition); omitting `--cut` computes over the grouping's CURRENT membership instead, which is the wrong set for a fresh approval — see the precondition above. This tool is read-only and asserts nothing about `status`/`pm_utterance` — printing a digest is not an approval. If you cannot obtain a computed value, leave the grouping `pending` and say so, rather than writing a digest to make the block look finished. (On a plan with no `grouping_approvals` key, the equivalent precondition is the legacy per-row `pm_approved:` deferral-ratification flips in the `yaml plan-tasks` spine — read-tolerated exactly as before, not extended.) The op hashes whatever plan body is on disk at invocation time, and owns the hash and the frontmatter write atomically.

On execution-naming approval, with every plan-body edit already landed, invoke (PowerShell hosts: Shape W) `& "$env:COORDINATOR_SETTINGS_HOME\bin\review-exec-auth-stamp.cmd" stamp <plan-path> --by "PM" --note "<verbatim PM utterance>"` — it computes the plan-body hash (over the FINAL plan body after all integration) and writes all four `execution_authorized_{by,at,sha,note}` frontmatter fields in one atomic mutation; idempotent, so re-invoking with identical values is a no-op. Then `/handoff` (execution handoff) so a fresh session runs `/execute-plan`. Same-session `/execute-plan` is the token-economics carve-out only — narrow, named-reason, never the default. The two bypasses of this checkpoint: (a) the PM has explicitly post-review authorized execution under the token-economics carve-out, or (b) the session is in `/autonomous` mode (the `autonomous` skill writes `/tmp/autonomous-run-${SESSION_ID}`; the checkpoint is sentinel-keyed in consumers, and `/autonomous` bypasses to same-session `/execute-plan` without the handoff). Only after authorization does the EM own the dispatch-gate graph — for same-session execution under the carve-out or `/autonomous`; the default execution-handoff path hands the dispatch-gate graph to the fresh session that picks up via `/pickup` → `/execute-plan`.
