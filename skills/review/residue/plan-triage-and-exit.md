**Triage lens — artifact-shape tradeoff.**

- _Artifact-shape tradeoff?_ (architectural direction, scope question, sequencing call, file-organization call, abstraction boundary)
  → Surface to PM with finding + reasoning. Wait for direction.
  Plan reviews skew heavily toward this row — most plan findings are about *what to build*, not *how it's coded*.
  - _(i) YAGNI / scope-trim argument from reviewer?_ → **Always escalation, never auto-trim.** Even framed as tradeoff-free, YAGNI is a product decision. Surface to PM. _See `coordinator/snippets/em-operating-doctrine.md` § How to Decide._
  - _(ii) Refactor-over-patch signal?_ → Refactor is the default when AI is the implementer. Surface to PM with refactor proposal. _Rationale inlined rather than cited, because the reader may not have the global doctrine on disk:_ under agent orchestration a critical rewrite is often an afternoon rather than a quarter, so *"too big"* means genuinely complex, not merely large. The cost asymmetry that makes patching look like the conservative choice does not hold here.
  - _(iii) Build-vs-defer call?_ → Always PM. Never EM-unilateral. _See `coordinator/snippets/em-operating-doctrine.md` § How to Decide._

**Triage lens — premise / hypothesis question (`--surface plan`).**

- _Premise / hypothesis question?_ (reviewer challenges the artifact's framing or motivating hypothesis, or claims a bug/gap exists where the artifact is correct)
  → Read the cited prior art (wiki, lessons, archived spec) at its source, not the reviewer's paraphrase. Confirm or revise premise either way.

**Triage lens — worker dispatch recommendations (`--surface plan`).**

- _Worker Dispatch Recommendations block present in reviewer output?_
  → On plan reviews: `doc-link-checker` (most relevant); `dep-cve-auditor` (if plan introduces a dependency). `test-evidence-parser` and `security-audit-worker` do NOT fire for plan reviews — they require runtime artifacts; if a reviewer names them on a plan, treat as miscalibration and surface to PM.

**Prior-Art Mutability and Reviewer Elevation**

**Prior-art mutability as first-class deliverable.** When the PM authorizes prior-art mutation as an explicit deliverable of the review (i.e., the plan is intended to *update* settled doctrine, not simply comply with it), the director-elevated reviewer (typically the Director of Engineering) MAY override the prior-art-checker sidecar's `update-plan` / `update-prior-art` recommendation with an explicit cross-reference to the PM authorization in their findings. Default mode is still "plan adapts to prior art"; prior-art mutation is opt-in and requires PM sign-off stated in the dispatch brief.

**Reviewer elevation must be stated verbatim in the dispatch brief.** Elevating a reviewer past their charter (e.g., the Director of Engineering from ambition-backstop to director-with-mutation-authority) without verbatim brief language reverts at integrator-apply — the reviewer's prompt-defined charter is the default boundary. If the PM authorizes elevation, the EM must include the exact authorization phrase (e.g., "PM-authorized to override prior-art-checker on this run") in the dispatch brief to the reviewer; otherwise the integrator will treat the override as out-of-charter and escalate as ASK.

**Execution authorization gate.** When all reviewer integration is complete, surface to the PM and WAIT: _"Plan reviewed and integrated. Proceed to execute?"_ Review approval is not execution authorization — the EM does not begin dispatching on its own, and the write-bar is the PM's words, not their sentiment: the stamp op below fires ONLY when the PM's message NAMES execution ("execute", "proceed to execute", "run it", "go ahead and execute", or an unambiguous captain's-order "go" idiom — "make it so", "engage", "hit it", "let's fly"). Note "ship it" is deliberately NOT on this list — it is colloquially ambiguous (can mean merge/ship a diff, not execute a plan) and is judged by intent in context, the "ship it" that DOES fire a gate is the distinct review-WAIVER at reviewer selection ("ship it / skip review"). Plan-approval words alone ("looks good", "lgtm", "approved", "nice") authorize the PLAN, NOT its execution, and MUST NOT fire the stamp op.

**The deferral slate rides with this gate, argued not listed.** If the plan carries candidate scope cuts (rows `open` and carrying `case_against`, or already `backlogged`/`wont_do`), the same ask surfaces them alongside execution — per candidate cut, the case for, the case against, and the EM's recommendation with confidence. **Presenting the slate as an ID list ("ratify D1 to D3?") is the failure mode, named explicitly here.** The evidence is this plan's own authoring: an EM's live application of the both-sides format resolved three deferrals as two reversals and a reframe — none the way a bare ratify-prompt would have gone, because a ratify-prompt has no surface for the argument that changes the PM's mind. Above n=4 candidate cuts, the gate presents buckets and a spinoff proposal instead of an enumerated slate. The gate presents per-row arguments, never the derived bucket name: a grouping approval goes to the PM as its rows, never as the bucket name, because the bucket name is the EM's own derivation and approving it approves a summary nobody argued. Candidate-cut rows are authored `open` and flipped to `backlogged`/`wont_do` by `plan_tasks.mutate` only AFTER the PM rules here — the write-time guard already enforces that ordering; this gate is the ratification moment it is enforcing against.

**Precondition — land every plan-body edit the approval entails BEFORE invoking the op.** On a plan whose frontmatter carries a `grouping_approvals` key, that means the `do` / `defer` / `ruled_out` grouping block(s) the PM just approved: `status: approved`, the `approver`, `approved_at`, the PM's verbatim `pm_utterance`, and the membership `digest` for that grouping — plus the plan-header `Status:` line and any final integration note. **The `digest` is the one field in that block you never author from your own head.** The other four are the PM's words and the clock; the digest is a mechanical sha256 over the grouping's membership set, and a value that is merely plausible is worse than no value — it binds the approval to a cut-set nobody approved, and the write-time guard will reject every later close against it. Take it from `"${COORDINATOR_SETTINGS_HOME:-$HOME/.coordinator-claude-settings}/bin/plan-tasks-grouping-digest" --plan <plan-path> --grouping do|defer|ruled_out --cut <id>:<disposition>[,<id>:<disposition>...]` — it prints the digest over the membership the cut-set produces, never from an estimate of what the recipe would say. Pass `--cut` for every row the PM's cut-set is closing (with its target disposition); omitting `--cut` computes over the grouping's CURRENT membership instead, which is the wrong set for a fresh approval — see the precondition above. This tool is read-only: it never opens the plan for write and never checks or asserts `status`/`pm_utterance` — printing a digest is not itself an approval, only the mechanical value one field of an already-PM-approved block needs. If you cannot obtain a computed value, leave the grouping `pending` and say so, rather than writing a digest to make the block look finished. (On a plan with no `grouping_approvals` key, the equivalent precondition is the legacy per-row `pm_approved:` deferral-ratification flips in the `yaml plan-tasks` spine — read-tolerated exactly as before, not extended.) This ordering is the one judgment residue left here (the op hashes whatever plan body is on disk at invocation time); hash computation and the frontmatter write are not narrated steps here — they are owned atomically by the op itself.

Why the ordering matters, spelled out because it is easy to miss: the execution-authorization stamp binds a hash of the plan body as it stands at invocation. If any approval-state edit lands AFTER that hash is computed, the stamp ends up describing a plan body that does not exist — the same failure mode a grouping's own membership digest exists to guard against at its own altitude (a digest computed over stale membership fails to match the rows it's supposed to attest to). Land the approval edits first, then invoke the op.

On execution-naming approval, with every plan-body edit already landed, invoke `"${COORDINATOR_SETTINGS_HOME:-$HOME/.coordinator-claude-settings}/bin/review-exec-auth-stamp" stamp <plan-path> --by "PM" --note "<verbatim PM utterance>"` — it computes the plan-body hash (over the FINAL plan body after all integration) and writes all four `execution_authorized_{by,at,sha,note}` frontmatter fields in one atomic mutation; idempotent, so re-invoking with identical values is a no-op. Then `/handoff` (execution handoff) so a fresh session runs `/execute-plan`. Same-session `/execute-plan` is the token-economics carve-out only — narrow, named-reason, never the default. The two bypasses of this checkpoint: (a) the PM has explicitly post-review authorized execution under the token-economics carve-out, or (b) the session is in `/autonomous` mode (the `autonomous` skill writes `/tmp/autonomous-run-${SESSION_ID}`; the checkpoint is sentinel-keyed in consumers, and `/autonomous` bypasses to same-session `/execute-plan` without the handoff). Only after authorization does the EM own the dispatch-gate graph — for same-session execution under the carve-out or `/autonomous`; the default execution-handoff path hands the dispatch-gate graph to the fresh session that picks up via `/pickup` → `/execute-plan`.
